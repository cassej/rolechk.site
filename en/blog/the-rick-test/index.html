The Rick Sanchez Test: 5 Signs You're a Visionary (and Your Business Is on Fire)

Article 2 of the series. A brutal, cynical, but damn truthful post-mortem for those stuck in "I'll figure it out myself" mode.

In the last episode, we figured out that trying to drag a company alone is mental suicide. Today, we take off the masks. Come in, have a seat at the operating table. We're going to dissect your brain to find out just how close you are to the archetypal mad genius — Rick Sanchez.

The book Rocket Fuel claims that only about 3–5% of people possess true entrepreneurial vision. If you started a business, chances are you're one of them. But this coin has a very ugly flip side.

Let's be honest. How many times have you caught yourself thinking your team is a bunch of lazy Jerrys who can't take a step without your direction? Now look in the mirror. Maybe the problem isn't them. Maybe you're just consuming their resources with your chaos.

Here are 5 classic signs you're a Visionary. Check yourself before your business collapses into a black hole.

1. "I've got an idea!" every 10 minutes

Your brain is a nuclear reactor generating insights at industrial scale. You're watering a plant, watching a show, driving a car — and BAM! New product, new market, new positioning strategy.

You burst into the work chat with burning eyes: "Morty, drop everything, we're going to rob Pluto!" The team obediently drops current tasks, rebuilds processes, spends a week on testing... and next Monday you show up and say: "Pluto sucks, I came up with something way cooler!"

Diagnosis: You inspire, but you also paralyze. Constant course-changing — aka Shiny Object Syndrome — turns your company into a boat spinning wildly in place.

2. Allergy to micromanagement and spreadsheets

The mere sight of an Excel sheet makes you nauseous. Filling out a CRM triggers an existential crisis comparable to realizing the meaninglessness of the universe. You genuinely don't understand why you need to spend two hours writing up procedures when "it's totally obvious, just do it properly!"

Your element is chaos, storming, firefighting in "hero mode," and closing big deals through sheer charisma.

Diagnosis: A business only grows when it has a system. Without systems, you're not building a corporation — you're building a nomad camp. If you're not there, the camp dies.

3. Your employees fear you (and don't understand you)

When you walk into the office or join a call, the temperature drops. No, you're not a evil tyrant. You just think three light-years ahead. You give a task like: "We need to rebuild the funnel so the customer feels at home."

To you, that's a crystal-clear picture. To your marketer, it's a collection of vague sounds. They do it wrong, you blow up, scream that everyone's an idiot, and retreat to your garage.

Diagnosis: Visionaries often have a broken communication gateway. You live in the future; your team lives in the present. They need rails and metrics, not your cosmic metaphors.

4. You abandon projects at 80% completion

You're insanely excited about the start. First sales, concept development, choosing a logo, shouts of "We're going to crush the market!" But as soon as it comes to routine implementation, bug fixing, legal red tape, and integration setup — your fuse goes out. You get bored. The task hangs at "almost done" for weeks while you've already jumped to a new "shiny object."

Diagnosis: You're a sprinter, but business is a marathon. Without someone who methodically pushes tasks to 100% completion, your company becomes a graveyard of brilliant but non-functional prototypes.

5. You're the main bottleneck of your own company

The most paradoxical one. You created this business, but you're also preventing it from growing. All key decisions are locked on you. The designer can't publish a banner without your "okay." The salesperson can't give a discount without your approval. You feel like a slave to your own creation, working 14-hour days, but if you turn off your phone for two days — everything stops and burns.

Diagnosis: You've tied operations to your personality because you subconsciously fear letting go of the wheel. You confuse control over the company with control over processes.

How many out of 5 did you score?

If at least 3 out of 5 — congratulations, you're a pure-blooded Rick Sanchez. Your brain is a billion-dollar asset, but your management methods are pure liability.

The good news: you don't need to break yourself, learn to love spreadsheets, or force yourself to check reports. That won't work — you'll just burn out and hate your work. Your job is to stay Rick, but find someone to handle the grunt work. Someone to clean up your garage.

In the next article, we'll break down who a real Integrator is. Spoiler: it's not an obedient secretary or just a COO. It's someone who has the right to tell you "Shut up, Rick, we're sticking to the plan." Ready for that? Wait for the next portal gun signal.
